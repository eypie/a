Solid State Physics: Unit 1

Foundation of Materials Science — 3D Geometry with Higher Stakes

1. Bonding in Solids

Definitions:

  • Lattice Energy: The energy required to separate one mole of a solid into its constituent gaseous ions.
  • Primary Bonds: Strong chemical bonds (Ionic, Covalent, Metallic) involving the sharing or transfer of electrons.
  • Secondary Bonds: Weaker physical bonds (Van der Waals, Hydrogen) arising from dipole interactions.

Key Formulas:

The force \(F\) between two atoms at a distance \(r\) is the negative gradient of the potential energy \(U\):

$$F = -\frac{dU}{dr}$$

At the equilibrium distance \(r_0\), the net force is zero (\(F=0\)), and the potential energy \(U\) is at its minimum.

2. Crystal Structure Fundamentals

Definitions:

  • Space Lattice: An infinite periodic array of points in space.
  • Basis (Motif): A group of one or more atoms associated with each lattice point.
  • Crystal Structure: Lattice + Basis.
  • Unit Cell: The smallest repeating unit of a crystal that carries the full symmetry of the lattice.
  • Primitive Cell: A unit cell that contains exactly one lattice point.
  • Coordination Number (CN): The number of nearest neighbors surrounding a specific atom.

Atomic Packing Fraction (APF):

The ratio of the volume occupied by atoms in a unit cell to the total volume of the unit cell.

$$APF = \frac{N \times \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3}{a^3}$$

(Where \(N\) is the number of effective atoms, \(r\) is the atomic radius, and \(a\) is the lattice constant.)

3. Crystallography & Miller Indices

Definitions:

  • Miller Indices \((hkl)\): A symbolic vector representation for the orientation of atomic planes in a crystal lattice.
  • Reciprocal Lattice: A conceptual lattice where each point corresponds to a set of planes in the real lattice. It simplifies the geometry of diffraction.

Key Formulas:

  • Interplanar Spacing (\(d_{hkl}\)): For a cubic system with lattice constant \(a\):
$$d_{hkl} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2 + l^2}}$$
  • Reciprocal Lattice Vectors:
$$\vec{a^*} = 2\pi \frac{\vec{b} \times \vec{c}}{\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})}$$

(Note: The reciprocal of an FCC lattice is a BCC lattice, and vice versa.)

4. X-Ray Diffraction (XRD)

Definitions:

  • Bragg's Law: The condition for constructive interference of waves scattered from successive planes of a crystal.
  • Crystalline: Atoms arranged in a long-range periodic order.
  • Polycrystalline: Made of many small crystals (grains) in random orientations.
  • Amorphous: No long-range order (e.g., glass).

Key Formulas:

  • Bragg's Law:
$$n\lambda = 2d \sin \theta$$

(Where \(n\) is the order of reflection, \(\lambda\) is wavelength, \(d\) is interplanar spacing, and \(\theta\) is the glancing angle.)

  • Lattice Constant from Density:
$$a = \left( \frac{N \times M}{\rho \times N_A} \right)^{1/3}$$

(Where \(\rho\) is density, \(M\) is molecular weight, and \(N_A\) is Avogadro's number.)

  • Scherrer Formula: Used to estimate the size of very small crystals (crystallite size \(D\)):
$$D = \frac{K \lambda}{\beta \cos \theta}$$

(Where \(K\) is a dimensionless shape factor (~0.9) and \(\beta\) is the line broadening at half the maximum intensity, FWHM.)

5. Diffraction Types (Qualitative)

  • X-Ray Diffraction: Interacts with electron clouds; standard for crystal structure.
  • Electron Diffraction: High scattering power; used for thin films and surfaces (requires vacuum).
  • Neutron Diffraction: Interacts with the nucleus; ideal for locating light atoms (like Hydrogen) or studying magnetic properties.
💡 Quick Tip: When solving Miller Indices problems, if a plane is parallel to an axis, the intercept is \(\infty\), and the index becomes \(0\) (since \(1/\infty = 0\)).
