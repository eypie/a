UNIT - I

1. (a) Derive de-Broglie's Equation. How does this equation explain the dual nature of wave? [2½+1½]

Derivation: From Planck's theory, $E = h\nu = hc/\lambda$. From Einstein, $E = mc^2$. Equating them: $mc^2 = hc/\lambda$, which simplifies to $\lambda = h/mc$. Replacing $c$ with $v$ for a material particle, we get $\lambda = h/mv = h/p$. This equation relates the wave nature ($\lambda$) to the particle nature ($p$), demonstrating the dual nature of matter.

1. (b) Calculate the velocity and energy of an electron if the de-Broglie wavelength is $10^{-10}m$. [2½]

Calculation: Using $v = h/(m\lambda)$, where $h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} J\cdot s$ and $m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} kg$:
$v = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} / (9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 10^{-10}) = 7.273 \times 10^6 m/s$.
Energy $E = 1/2 mv^2 = 0.5 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times (7.273 \times 10^6)^2 \approx 2.41 \times 10^{-17} J$.

2. Write short notes on: (i) Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (ii) Hamiltonian operator (iii) Postulates of Quantum Mechanics. [7]

(i) Heisenberg's Principle: $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \geq h/4\pi$. It is impossible to know both position and momentum of a particle simultaneously with perfect precision.

(ii) Hamiltonian Operator: Represents total energy; $\hat{H} = \hat{T} + \hat{V}$. It is used in the Schrödinger equation $\hat{H}\psi = E\psi$.

(iii) Postulates: Define the state of a system via wave function $\psi$, assign linear operators to observables, and dictate that measurement yields eigenvalues.

UNIT - II

3. Explain Molecular Orbital Theory for $H_2$. Discuss its superiority over VBT. [7]

In MOT, $1s$ orbitals of two H atoms overlap to form a bonding ($\sigma$) and an antibonding ($\sigma^*$) orbital. The electrons occupy the lower-energy $\sigma$ orbital. MOT is superior because it successfully predicts the paramagnetic nature of $O_2$ and explains molecular stability using bond orders, which VBT fails to address.

UNIT - III

6. (b) Prove $E_j = \overline{B}J(J+1)cm^{-1}$. Give selection rules. [4]

Starting from $E_J = h^2 / (8\pi^2 I) \cdot J(J+1)$ Joules, we convert to $cm^{-1}$ by dividing by $hc$:
$E_J = h / (8\pi^2 Ic) \cdot J(J+1)$.
Defining $\overline{B} = h / (8\pi^2 Ic)$, we get $E_J = \overline{B}J(J+1) cm^{-1}$. Selection rule: $\Delta J = \pm 1$.

UNIT - V

9. (b) Calculate molar mass of a substance if 10g in 250ml water has $\pi = 600 mm Hg$ at 15°C. [4]

Using $\pi V = (W/M)RT$:
$\pi = 600/760 \approx 0.7895 atm$, $V = 0.25 L$, $T = 288 K$, $R = 0.0821$.
$M = (10 \times 0.0821 \times 288) / (0.7895 \times 0.25) \approx 1198 g/mol$.
