🌟 UNIT-II: BAND THEORY OF SOLIDS + SEMICONDUCTORS

SHORT NOTES — Exam-Ready Quick Revision

🔷 1. BAND THEORY OF SOLIDS

🔹 1.1 Formation of Energy Bands

📌 Idea:
  • In an isolated atom → electrons have discrete energy levels
  • In a solid (many atoms close together) → Energy levels split and form bands
📌 Why splitting happens?
Because of:
  • Overlapping electron wave functions
  • Interaction between neighboring atoms
📌 Result:
Large number of atoms → very closely spaced energy levels → forms Energy Bands

🔹 Definitions

✔️ Energy Band: A continuous range of allowed electron energies.
✔️ Forbidden Gap (Band Gap): Energy region where no electron states exist

🔹 Important Bands

  • Valence Band (VB) → filled with electrons
  • Conduction Band (CB) → electrons can move freely
  • Band Gap (Eg) → gap between VB and CB

🔷 1.2 Periodic Potential & Bloch Theorem

📌 Concept:
In crystals: Atoms arranged in periodic lattice
So potential is periodic: \[ V(x) = V(x + a) \]

✔️ Bloch Theorem (VERY IMPORTANT)

📌 Statement:
Electron wave function in periodic potential is:
\[ \psi(x) = e^{ikx} u(x) \]
where:
  • \( u(x) \) is periodic: \( u(x+a)=u(x) \)
  • \( k \) = wave vector
📌 Meaning:
Electron behaves like:
  • free particle (via \( e^{ikx} \))
  • influenced by lattice (via \( u(x) \))

🔷 1.3 Number of States in a Band

\[ \text{Number of states} = 2N \]

where:

  • \( N \) = number of atoms
  • Factor 2 → due to spin

🔷 1.4 Kronig-Penney Model

📌 Purpose: Explains how bands and gaps form
📌 Assumption: Periodic potential → rectangular barriers
📌 Result: Gives relation: \[ \cos(ka) = f(E) \]
  • Allowed energies → when RHS lies between -1 and 1
  • Forbidden → otherwise
📌 Key Insight: Explains existence of allowed & forbidden bands

🔷 1.5 Brillouin Zones

📌 Definition: Region in k-space defining allowed electron wave vectors.
📌 First Brillouin Zone: \[ -\frac{\pi}{a} \le k \le \frac{\pi}{a} \]
📌 Importance:
  • Defines energy structure
  • Band edges occur at zone boundaries

🔷 1.6 Crystal Momentum

\[ p = \hbar k \]

where: \( \hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi} \)

📌 Meaning: Electron behaves like it has momentum inside crystal

🔷 1.7 Effective Mass of Electron

\[ m^* = \frac{\hbar^2}{\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}} \]
📌 Meaning: Electron behaves as if it has different mass inside crystal
🔹 Physical Origin: Due to interaction with lattice

🔹 Negative Effective Mass

📌 When: \[ \frac{d^2E}{dk^2} < 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad m^* < 0 \]
📌 Meaning: Electron moves opposite to applied force

🔹 Holes

📌 Definition: Absence of electron behaves like positive charge
📌 Properties:
  • Positive charge
  • Positive effective mass

🔷 1.8 Energy Dispersion Relations

✔️ (A) Weak Binding Approximation

📌 Idea: Electrons almost free
📌 Result: Slight modification of free electron energy \[ E = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m} \]

✔️ (B) Tight Binding Approximation

📌 Idea: Electrons strongly bound to atoms
📌 Result: \[ E(k) = E_0 - A\cos(ka) \]

🔷 2. SEMICONDUCTORS

🔹 2.1 Energy Band Structures

✔️ Conductors (Metals):
  • VB and CB overlap
  • \( E_g = 0 \)
✔️ Insulators:
  • Large band gap (~5 eV)
  • No conduction
✔️ Semiconductors:
  • Small band gap (~1 eV)

🔹 2.2 Direct vs Indirect Band Gap

✔️ Direct Band Gap
  • Condition: CB minimum and VB maximum at same k
  • Example: GaAs
✔️ Indirect Band Gap
  • Condition: CB minimum and VB maximum at different k
  • Example: Silicon

🔹 2.3 Generation & Recombination

✔️ Generation
Electron jumps VB → CB
  • Causes: Heat, Light
✔️ Recombination
Electron falls back to VB

🔹 2.4 Mobility

\[ \mu = \frac{v_d}{E} \]

where:

  • \( v_d \) = drift velocity
  • \( E \) = electric field

🔹 2.5 Hall Effect

✔️ Concept: Magnetic field causes charge separation
✔️ Hall Voltage: Voltage across conductor perpendicular to current
✔️ Hall Coefficient \[ R_H = \frac{1}{ne} \]
✔️ Charge Carrier Concentration \[ n = \frac{1}{eR_H} \]
✔️ Mobility (Hall Mobility) \[ \mu = \sigma R_H \]
✔️ Conductivity \[ \sigma = ne\mu \]
✔️ Hall Angle \[ \tan\theta = \mu B \]

🔷 Summary (Quick Revision)

  • Bands form due to interaction of atoms
  • Bloch theorem → electron wave inside periodic lattice
  • Effective mass → modified electron behavior
  • Negative mass → leads to holes
  • Weak binding → free electron-like
  • Tight binding → localized electron
  • Semiconductors → small band gap
  • Hall effect → gives carrier type, concentration, mobility
